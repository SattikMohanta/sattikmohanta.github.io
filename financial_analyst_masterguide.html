01

Financial Markets Foundations

How capital markets work, market participants, instruments, and behavioral finance. Based on Yale's Shiller course + Wharton's Intro to Corp Finance.

Market Structure

Financial markets facilitate the transfer of funds between savers and borrowers. They price risk, provide liquidity, and aggregate information. Understanding structure explains why prices move.

Market Types
  • Primary Market: New securities issued (IPO, bond issuance)
  • Secondary Market: Existing securities traded (NSE, BSE, NYSE)
  • OTC Market: Bilateral contracts — derivatives, forex, bonds
  • Money Market: Short-term instruments (<1 year) — T-bills, CP, CD
  • Capital Market: Long-term instruments — equities, bonds

Key Market Participants

  • Buy Side: Mutual funds, hedge funds, insurance cos, pension funds — they buy securities for portfolios
  • Sell Side: Investment banks, brokers — they sell research, underwrite, and facilitate transactions
  • Market Makers: Provide liquidity by quoting bid/ask; profit from spread
  • Retail Investors: Individuals; typically price-takers
  • Regulators: SEBI (India), SEC (US), RBI — rule setters

Core Financial Instruments — Deep Dive

📈 Equities (Stocks) ▼

A stock represents a fractional ownership claim in a company. Shareholders have residual claim — they get what's left after all other creditors are paid.

Stock Return Components
Total Return = (P₁ - P₀ + D) / P₀
Where P₀ = Purchase Price, P₁ = Selling Price, D = Dividends Received

Key Equity Metrics: EPS (profit per share), P/E ratio (multiple investors pay per ₹1 of earnings), Book Value, Dividend Yield, Beta (volatility vs market).

🏢
Indian Context: NSE lists 2,000+ stocks. Nifty50 = top 50 by free-float market cap. FIIs (Foreign Institutional Investors) hold ~25% of NSE — their flows drive massive moves. Track FII/DII data on NSE website daily.
📊 Bonds & Fixed Income ▼

A bond is a debt instrument: the issuer borrows money from investors, pays periodic coupons, and returns principal at maturity. Bond prices move inversely to yields — this is the most fundamental fixed income concept.

Bond Price Formula
P = Σ [C / (1+y)ᵗ] + [FV / (1+y)ⁿ]
C = Coupon payment, y = yield to maturity, n = periods, FV = Face value (₹1000 typical)
Current Yield vs YTM
Current Yield = Annual Coupon / Current Price
YTM = Discount rate where NPV of all cash flows = Price
💡
Credit Quality Ratings: AAA → AA → A → BBB (Investment Grade) → BB → B → CCC (Junk). Indian G-Sec (Government Securities) are AAA. When RBI raises rates, existing bond prices fall — critical for FP&A and treasury functions.
🔷 Derivatives — Options, Futures, Swaps ▼

Derivatives derive their value from an underlying asset. They are used for hedging (reducing risk) and speculation (amplifying bets).

Call Option

Right to Buy

Buyer pays premium for the right (not obligation) to buy asset at strike price K before expiry. Profit = max(S - K - Premium, -Premium).

Intrinsic Value = max(S - K, 0)
Put Option

Right to Sell

Buyer has right to sell asset at K. Used to hedge downside. Long put = insurance on your equity position.

Intrinsic Value = max(K - S, 0)
Black-Scholes Model (Option Pricing)
C = S·N(d₁) - K·e^(-rT)·N(d₂) d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T) d₂ = d₁ - σ√T
S=Stock price, K=Strike, r=risk-free rate, T=time to expiry, σ=volatility, N=cumulative normal distribution. The Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta, Rho) are partial derivatives of this formula.
🧠 Behavioral Finance (Shiller's Framework) ▼

Robert Shiller argues markets are NOT always efficient — psychological biases cause bubbles and crashes. Understanding these biases helps you spot mispricing.

Bias 1

Overconfidence

Investors overestimate their ability to pick stocks. 90% of day traders lose money. Institutional traders who see themselves as "always right" blow up portfolios.

Bias 2

Herd Behavior

People follow the crowd. Caused the 2000 dot-com bubble (everyone buying internet stocks) and 2008 housing bubble. Shiller's CAPE ratio >30 signals bubble territory.

Bias 3

Loss Aversion

Losing ₹1000 feels 2× worse than gaining ₹1000 feels good (Kahneman & Tversky). Leads to holding losers too long and selling winners too early.

Bias 4

Recency Bias

Investors extrapolate recent trends. After 2008 crash, people avoided equities at the worst time — missing the decade-long bull run (SPX: 666 → 4,800).

🔍 Case Study: Shiller CAPE Ratio — Predicting Nifty Bubble
INDIA 2024
Situation: Nifty50 rallied from 8,000 (March 2020) to 26,000 (Sep 2024) — a 225% gain in 4 years. CAPE (Cyclically Adjusted P/E) hit 35x vs historical average of 22x.

Shiller Method: CAPE = Current Price / Average Real Earnings (inflation-adjusted) over 10 years. A CAPE of 35 means you're paying 35× average earnings — historically predicts below-average 10-year returns.

What happened: Smart money (FIIs) began selling ₹1.14 lakh crore Oct–Dec 2024. Nifty corrected 13% to ~22,500 by March 2025. DIIs (domestic institutions) absorbed selling but at stretched valuations.

Analyst Action: At CAPE >30, reduce equity allocation to 40-50% of portfolio. Rotate into defensive sectors (FMCG, pharma, utilities). This is Shiller's behavioral alpha.

Financial Statement Analysis

The three statements form the foundation of every valuation model. Master reading them before building any model.

Every public company files three core financial statements. An analyst's job is to identify what the numbers reveal about competitive position, earnings quality, and future cash generation.

📋 Income Statement (P&L) — Profitability ▼

The P&L shows revenues earned and costs incurred over a period. Key insight: Revenue − All Costs = Net Profit. Flow statement (not a snapshot).

What to Look For:

  • Revenue growth rate vs peers: Infosys 4.7% vs TCS 6.8% → Infosys losing market share
  • Gross Margin trend: If cost-of-revenues growing faster than revenue = margin compression
  • EBITDA margin: Infosys ~27% = industry-leading (TCS ~26%, Wipro ~22%)
  • Other Income: Large other income relative to EBIT = low quality earnings (not from core ops)
  • Effective tax rate: Significantly below 25-30% = tax optimization or one-time benefits
🏦 Balance Sheet — Financial Position ▼

The balance sheet is a snapshot of what the company owns (Assets) and who has a claim on those assets (Liabilities + Equity). The fundamental equation: Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity

Assets Side

What the Company Owns

  • Current Assets: Cash, Debtors (A/R), Inventory, Prepaid
  • Non-Current Assets: PP&E (plant, property, equipment), intangibles, goodwill, investments
  • Key insight: Rising inventory relative to revenue = demand problem. Rising A/R = collection issues.
Liabilities + Equity Side

Who Has a Claim

  • Current Liabilities: Creditors (A/P), short-term debt, accrued expenses
  • Long-term Debt: Bonds, term loans, debentures
  • Equity: Paid-up capital + retained earnings + reserves
  • Key insight: Debt/Equity ratio rising = increasing financial risk
⚠️
Analyst Red Flag: Goodwill on balance sheet = past acquisitions at premium. Large goodwill that gets impaired = management paid too much. Check goodwill / total assets ratio. If >40%, ask: what's it actually worth?
💵 Cash Flow Statement — King of Statements ▼

Warren Buffett: "Earnings can be managed; cash flows cannot be faked as easily." The CFS shows actual cash in/out across 3 activities:

Operating (CFO)

Core Business Cash

Net Income + D&A − ΔWorking Capital. Should be positive and growing. The most important number for quality assessment.

Investing (CFI)

Investment Activity

Capex (buying assets) = negative. Asset sales = positive. Heavy capex = growth investment (check ROI). Acquisitions appear here.

Financing (CFF)

Funding Activity

Debt raised/repaid, equity issued, dividends paid, buybacks. Negative CFF = returning cash to shareholders (positive sign for mature companies).

Free Cash Flow — The Most Critical Metric
FCF = CFO − Capex
FCFF (to Firm) = EBIT × (1-T) + D&A − ΔWC − Capex
FCFE (to Equity) = FCFF − Interest × (1-T) + Net Borrowing
FCF is what's available to pay dividends, buy back stock, make acquisitions, or pay down debt. A company with high net income but negative FCF is a red flag (see: IL&FS, Yes Bank).
🔍 Case: Reliance Industries — CFO vs EBITDA Check
FY2024
Reliance Industries FY2024: EBITDA = ₹1,81,000 Cr | CFO = ₹1,52,000 Cr | Capex = ₹1,35,000 Cr | FCF = ₹17,000 Cr (CFO−Capex).

Observation: CFO/EBITDA = 84% — good quality. Very high Capex (₹1.35L cr) reflects massive ongoing investment in O2C, Jio telecom, Retail, and Green Energy. Despite low FCF, this is justified reinvestment — not a red flag — because RIL is in hyper-growth phase (5G + solar).

Analyst Judgment: Separate "maintenance capex" (~₹20-25k cr) from "growth capex" (~₹1.1L cr). Maintenance capex FCF = ₹1.52L − ₹0.25L = ₹1.27L Cr — incredibly strong underlying business.

🔗 How the Three Statements Link

The three statements are fully integrated — this is the backbone of a 3-statement financial model:

1
Net Income flows to Equity
Net Income from P&L → adds to Retained Earnings on Balance Sheet. Dividends paid reduce retained earnings.
2
D&A reconciles P&L to Cash Flow
D&A is a non-cash expense on P&L (reduces profit) but added back on CFS. That's why CFS = Net Income + D&A as starting point.
3
Net Cash change hits Balance Sheet
Ending cash on CFS = Cash on Balance Sheet. This is the check that your model is correct ("does the balance sheet balance?").
4
Balance Sheet changes drive CFS
Increase in Debtors (A/R) = cash NOT yet received = negative on CFS. Decrease in Creditors (A/P) = paying suppliers faster = negative on CFS.

Ratio Analysis — Quantifying Performance

Ratios translate raw numbers into comparable insights. Ratios only have meaning in context: vs history, vs peers, vs industry.

Profitability Ratios

Margin Analysis
Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100
EBITDA Margin = EBITDA / Revenue × 100
Net Profit Margin = PAT / Revenue × 100
Return Ratios
ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity × 100
ROA = Net Income / Total Assets × 100
ROCE = EBIT / Capital Employed × 100
Capital Employed = Total Assets − Current Liabilities

DuPont Decomposition of ROE

DuPont breaks ROE into 3 drivers — crucial for understanding WHERE returns come from:

3-Factor DuPont
ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier = (NI/Rev) × (Rev/Assets) × (Assets/Equity)
A company can have high ROE through: (1) high margins (Apple, Infosys), (2) high asset efficiency (Walmart, D-Mart), or (3) high leverage (banks, airlines). Understand WHICH driver before comparing.
DuPont: HDFC Bank FY2024
BANKING
Net Margin: 22.1% | Asset Turnover: 0.08× (banks have huge asset bases) | Equity Multiplier: 9.2× (leverage)
ROE = 22.1% × 0.08 × 9.2 = 16.3% — excellent for a bank. Key driver: leverage, not margin. Risk: if asset quality deteriorates, leverage amplifies losses.

Liquidity & Solvency Ratios

DCF Valuation — Building from Scratch

The Discounted Cash Flow model is the gold standard of intrinsic valuation. Every IB/PE/hedge fund interview will test this. Master it completely.

💡
DCF Philosophy: A business is worth the present value of all future cash flows it will generate. We forecast those cash flows, then discount them back at a rate that reflects risk (WACC). This is Discounted Cash Flow analysis.

The 7-Step DCF Process

1
Build Revenue & EBITDA Forecast (5–10 Years)
Project revenue using: (a) historical growth rate, (b) industry growth, (c) management guidance. Forecast margins based on competitive position and operating leverage. For cyclical industries, use mid-cycle margins, not peak.
2
Calculate FCFF Each Year
FCFF = EBIT × (1-T) + D&A − ΔNon-Cash Working Capital − Capex
Working capital changes: Rising NWC = cash consumed. Capex: split maintenance (sustaining business) vs growth (expanding capacity).
3
Calculate WACC (Discount Rate)
WACC = (E/V) × Ke + (D/V) × Kd × (1-T) Ke (CAPM) = Rf + β × (Rm - Rf) Rm - Rf = Equity Risk Premium (India: ~8-9%, US: ~5-6%)
Use 10-year G-Sec yield as Rf (India: ~7.2% in 2024). Beta from NSE. Kd = Weighted average cost of borrowings from annual report.
4
Calculate Terminal Value
TV (Gordon Growth) = FCFFₙ × (1+g) / (WACC - g) TV (Exit Multiple) = EBITDAₙ × EV/EBITDA multiple
Terminal value often = 60-80% of total EV. Critically test your terminal growth rate assumption (g should not exceed long-run GDP growth = ~6-7% for India, ~2.5% for US). Always cross-check both methods.
5
Discount All Cash Flows to Present Value
PV(FCFFₜ) = FCFFₜ / (1 + WACC)ᵗ Enterprise Value = Σ PV(FCFFₜ) + PV(Terminal Value)
6
Bridge EV to Equity Value (Equity Bridge)
Equity Value = EV − Net Debt − Minorities + Cash & Equivalents Intrinsic Share Price = Equity Value / Diluted Shares Outstanding
7
Sensitivity Analysis & Football Field Chart
Build a data table varying WACC (±1%) and Terminal Growth Rate (±0.5%). Present valuation as a range, not a point. The football field chart shows your valuation vs comps, precedents, 52-week range — standard in every pitchbook.
🔍 Complete DCF: Infosys Ltd — FY2024 Analyst Model

⚠️ DCF Pitfalls Every Analyst Must Know

Common Error 1

Terminal Value Dominates

If TV >80% of EV, your model is fragile. Small changes in g or WACC swing value 30%+. Always test: what EV/EBITDA exit multiple does your TV imply? If 40×, you've made an error.

Common Error 2

Circular WACC Reference

WACC uses target capital structure (market values), not book values. Don't use book D/E. Iterate: use assumed capital structure, compute EV, use that to get new D/E, recalculate. Usually 2–3 iterations converge.

Common Error 3

Forgetting Mid-Year Convention

Cash flows happen throughout the year, not at year end. Discount factor = 1/(1+WACC)^(t−0.5) for mid-year convention. This matters most in high-WACC, short-horizon models.

Common Error 4

Not Bridging to Equity Correctly

EV includes ALL capital providers. Subtract: gross debt, unfunded pension, minority interest, preferred equity. Add: cash & equivalents, financial investments. Then divide by fully diluted shares (include options, warrants, ESOP).

Comparable Company Analysis & Precedent Transactions

Relative valuation anchors your DCF. No valuation is complete without comps. This is what bankers do 80% of the time.

Trading Comps (Public Market Multiples)

Value a company based on how the public market prices similar companies. The multiples represent what rational investors are willing to pay right now.

Key Multiples
  • EV/EBITDA: Most commonly used in M&A (capital structure neutral)
  • EV/Revenue: For high-growth / unprofitable companies (SaaS, startups)
  • P/E: Equity-level multiple. Sensitive to capital structure — use with caution
  • EV/EBIT: Better than EBITDA for capex-light businesses
  • P/B: Price-to-book — gold standard for banks, NBFCs

The "GIGO" Problem in Comps

Garbage In, Garbage Out. Your comp set must be truly comparable. Selection criteria matter more than the math.

Peer Selection Criteria
  • Business model: Same product/service, value chain position
  • Size: Revenue/EBITDA within 0.3× to 3× of target
  • Geography: Same country or similar market maturity
  • Growth profile: Similar revenue growth rate ±5%
  • Capital structure: Use EV-based multiples to neutralize debt differences

Leveraged Buyout (LBO) Model

The crown jewel of PE finance. An LBO uses debt to amplify equity returns. Mastering LBO modeling is mandatory for PE and IB roles.

🎯
LBO Logic: A PE firm uses ~30% equity + ~70% debt to buy a company. The target's own cash flows repay the debt over 5 years. When PE sells (exit), the equity slice is now worth the entire value less (much smaller) remaining debt. Leverage amplifies equity returns — this is the magic of LBO.

LBO Return Mechanics

IRR Drivers in an LBO
Equity Return = f(Entry Multiple, Exit Multiple, EBITDA Growth, Debt Paydown, Dividends)
MoM (Money-on-Money) = Exit Equity Value / Entry Equity Check
IRR: Solve for r → Entry Equity = Exit Equity / (1+r)^n
PE funds typically target 20%+ IRR and 2-3× MoM over 5 years. Sources of return: ~40% from EBITDA growth, ~30% from debt paydown, ~30% from multiple expansion.
🔍 LBO Case Study: Hypothetical PE Buyout of ABC Pharma India
BUILD-FROM-SCRATCH
Situation: KKR considers buying ABC Pharma, a specialty generics manufacturer. FY24 EBITDA: ₹500 Cr. Entry multiple: 12× EV/EBITDA. Management forecasts 10% EBITDA CAGR over 5 years.

Entry:
Enterprise Value = 12× × ₹500 = ₹6,000 Cr
Net Debt assumed = ₹600 Cr → Equity Value = ₹5,400 Cr
Funding: 65% Debt (₹3,900 Cr) + 35% Equity (₹2,100 Cr = KKR's equity check)

5-Year Projection:
EBITDA grows to ₹805 Cr (10% CAGR). Debt repaid from FCF → Remaining debt at exit: ₹1,400 Cr.

Exit (Year 5):
Exit multiple = 13× (slight expansion due to scale + margin improvement)
Exit EV = 13× × ₹805 = ₹10,465 Cr
Exit Equity = ₹10,465 − ₹1,400 = ₹9,065 Cr

Returns:
MoM = ₹9,065 / ₹2,100 = 4.3× (excellent, target = 2.5–3×)
IRR = solve: ₹2,100 × (1+r)⁵ = ₹9,065 → r = 33.9% (well above 20% hurdle)

Sensitivity: If exit multiple = 10× (compression), IRR = 22%. Still viable. If EBITDA grows only 5% CAGR + 10× exit → IRR = 15% → below hurdle → DON'T BUY.

LBO Candidate Screening Checklist

Ideal LBO Target Characteristics
  • ✅ Stable, predictable FCF: Debt servicing requires consistent cash. DSCR >1.5× through cycle
  • ✅ Low existing leverage: Need room to add 4–6× Net Debt/EBITDA without distress
  • ✅ Defensible market position: Barriers to entry prevent margin erosion during debt paydown period
  • ✅ Tangible assets: PP&E that can be pledged as collateral to secure debt
  • ✅ Operational improvement potential: Cost savings, margin expansion, working capital release PE can drive
  • ❌ Avoid: Cyclical businesses, R&D-intensive, rapid disruption risk

Integrated 3-Statement Financial Model

The 3-statement model is the foundation of every DCF, LBO, and M&A model. Building it from scratch is the single most tested skill in finance interviews.

Architecture of a 3-Statement Model

A properly built 3-statement model has a single set of assumptions that drives everything else dynamically. Change one input → all three statements update automatically. This is called a fully integrated model.

1
Inputs & Assumptions Tab
Revenue growth %, EBITDA margin %, D&A as % of revenue, tax rate, capex as % of revenue, NWC days (DSO, DIO, DPO), interest rate on debt. ALL assumptions here. NEVER hardcode numbers into calculations.
2
Income Statement
Revenue → EBITDA → EBIT → PBT → Net Income. Each line driven by assumption. Tax = PBT × tax rate. Check deferred taxes if doing detailed work.
3
Balance Sheet — Working Capital Schedule
Accounts Receivable = Revenue / 365 × DSO. Inventory = COGS / 365 × DIO. Accounts Payable = COGS / 365 × DPO. ΔWC = ΔCA − ΔCL (used in CFS).
4
PP&E & Debt Schedules
PP&E: Opening + Capex − D&A = Closing. Debt: Opening + New Borrowings − Repayments = Closing. Interest = Opening Debt × interest rate (or average balance for circularity). Revolving credit facility = plug for cash shortfall.
5
Cash Flow Statement
CFO = Net Income + D&A − ΔNon-cash WC. CFI = −Capex ± acquisitions. CFF = net debt change ± equity issuance − dividends. Net cash change + opening cash = closing cash.
6
Balance Sheet Completion & Check
Equity = Prior Equity + Net Income − Dividends. Cash from CFS flows to BS. The check cell: Assets − Liabilities − Equity = 0. If this doesn't balance, you have a formula error. Fix before proceeding.
🛠️
Excel Pro Tips for 3-Statement Models: Use separate colored cells (blue = hardcode, black = formula, green = link from other sheet). Use Ctrl+[ to trace precedents. Use F5 → Special → Constants to find hardcodes. Name your ranges. Use IFERROR to handle divide-by-zero. Circular references in interest calculations → enable iterative calculation (File → Options → Formulas).

Credit Risk Scoring Model

Assess probability of default using financial and behavioral data. Essential for banking, NBFC, and risk roles.

🏦
Context: Indian banks have battled NPAs (Non-Performing Assets) for a decade. Gross NPA ratio peaked at 11.2% in 2018. By 2024 it fell to 2.8% — driven by better credit scoring, IBC (Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code), and stricter monitoring. Credit analysis prevents the next IL&FS or Yes Bank.

The 5 Cs of Credit Analysis

Character

Willingness to Pay

Management track record, promoter integrity, governance standards. Check: Related party transactions, promoter pledging, litigation history.

Capacity

Ability to Pay

DSCR, interest coverage, FCF adequacy. Primary repayment source = operating cash flow. DSCR >1.25× minimum; 1.5× comfortable.

Capital

Financial Cushion

Net worth / equity base. Leverage ratios. Covenant headroom. How much can go wrong before debt can't be repaid?

Collateral

Secondary Protection

Assets pledged as security. LTV (Loan-to-Value) ratio. Liquidity of collateral. Real estate, plant, inventory, debtors.

Conditions

External Environment

Industry cyclicality, macroeconomic outlook, regulatory risk, competitive threats. Credit for a steel company vs IT company = very different risk profiles.

Credit Score Model: Z-Score (Altman)

Altman Z-Score — Bankruptcy Prediction Model
Z = 1.2×X₁ + 1.4×X₂ + 3.3×X₃ + 0.6×X₄ + 1.0×X₅ X₁ = Working Capital / Total Assets X₂ = Retained Earnings / Total Assets X₃ = EBIT / Total Assets X₄ = Market Cap / Book Value of Debt X₅ = Revenue / Total Assets
Z > 2.99 = Safe zone. 1.81–2.99 = Grey zone. Z < 1.81 = Distress zone (high bankruptcy risk). Developed for US manufacturing; apply with caution to Indian service sector — adjust weightings.
🔍 Z-Score Analysis: Yes Bank (2019 Warning Signals)
INDIA BANKING CRISIS
Timeline: Yes Bank's Gross NPA surged from 0.31% (FY16) to 18.87% (FY20) — a 60× increase in 4 years. The RBI finally imposed a moratorium in March 2020.

Warning Signals (detectable by 2018):
1. DSCR declining: Operating cash flow turned negative in FY19 despite reported profits → earnings quality issue
2. Divergence between RBI assessment and bank's NPA: RBI's Risk Based Supervision showed ₹3,277 Cr NPA gap in FY16 → management hiding bad loans
3. Concentration risk: Top 20 borrowers = 25% of loan book. Single-borrower concentration in real estate (Zee, DHFL)
4. Promoter pledging: Rana Kapoor pledged 79% of his shareholding → structural warning
5. Tier-1 Capital eroding: CET-1 fell to 6.3% vs RBI minimum 7.375% — capital inadequate to absorb losses

Model Lesson: Always check: CFO vs Net Income divergence, NPA trend vs regulatory assessment, promoter share pledging %, capital adequacy ratio.

M&A Deal Analysis

Pick a real acquisition and analyze strategic rationale, financial impact, synergies, and risks. The strongest project for IB and consulting roles.

M&A Analysis Framework

Strategic Rationale Analysis
  • Market expansion: New geographies, customer segments
  • Product/capability acquisition: Tech, IP, talent
  • Vertical integration: Capturing supplier or distributor margin
  • Consolidation: Reducing competition, pricing power
  • Diversification: Reducing business risk (often value-destroying)
Financial Impact Analysis
  • EPS Accretion/Dilution: Does deal increase or decrease acquirer EPS?
  • Goodwill created: Premium paid over fair value = intangible risk
  • Pro-forma leverage: Combined entity's Net Debt/EBITDA
  • Synergy NPV: Present value of cost savings + revenue synergies
  • Breakeven synergy: How much synergy needed to justify premium?
Accretion/Dilution Analysis
Accretion/Dilution = (New EPS - Old EPS) / Old EPS × 100% Post-deal EPS = (Acquirer NI + Target NI + Synergies − Amortization − Interest) / (Combined Diluted Shares) Deal is ACCRETIVE if Post-deal EPS > Pre-deal EPS
A deal funded by cash is typically more accretive (no share dilution). A deal funded by overvalued stock can be dilutive even with synergies. IB teams run "accretion/dilution models" for every potential deal structure (cash, stock, combination).
🔍 Live M&A Case: HDFC Bank ← HDFC Ltd Merger (2023)
₹40 LAKH CRORE DEAL
Deal: HDFC Bank absorbed parent HDFC Limited in July 2023. Largest merger in Indian corporate history. Shareholders of HDFC Ltd received 42 shares of HDFC Bank for every 25 shares of HDFC Ltd.

Strategic Rationale:
1. Regulatory unlock: As a bank, HDFC Bank couldn't deploy HDFC Ltd's mortgage book directly. Merger allows direct mortgage origination through bank branches (3,700+ outlets)
2. Cross-sell: HDFC Bank's 80M+ customers → massive mortgage upsell opportunity
3. Funding cost reduction: HDFC Ltd borrowed at 7-8%; HDFC Bank deposits cost 5-6% → 100-200bps saving on ₹7 lakh crore book = ₹7,000-14,000 Cr annual savings
4. Valuation premium capture: Bank P/B multiples (3-4×) > NBFC multiples (2.5×)

Challenges (analyst concerns):
- HDFC Bank's LDR (Loan-to-Deposit Ratio) jumped from 85% to 110% post-merger → needed ₹4 lakh crore deposits quickly
- NIM (Net Interest Margin) compressed from 4.3% to 3.4% — mortgage loans yield less than unsecured loans
- CRR & SLR requirements on new deposits = non-earning assets, drag on ROA

Stock performance: HDFC Bank underperformed Nifty Bank by 30%+ in 2024 — short-term pain from integration complexity and NIM compression. Long-term thesis intact.

Synergy Valuation Formula

Synergy NPV Calculation
Synergy NPV = Σ [Annual Synergies × (1-T)] / (1+WACC)ᵗ + TV Cost Synergies: Headcount reduction, facility consolidation, procurement savings Revenue Synergies: Cross-sell, pricing power, market access (typically 30-40% realized) Time to achieve: Typically 2-3 years for cost, 3-5 years for revenue synergies
Rule of thumb: Pay no more than 50% of synergy NPV as premium above standalone value. If you're paying 100% of synergies, you're gifting all value creation to target shareholders.

FP&A — Financial Planning & Analysis + Budgeting

The engine room of corporate finance. FP&A translates strategy into numbers and holds the business accountable to them.

The FP&A Annual Cycle

Q3: Strategic Plan

3-Year Vision

Top-down strategic direction from CEO/CFO. Market share targets, new businesses, capex programs. Finance translates to financial projections.

Q4: Annual Budget

Detailed 1-Year Plan

Bottom-up: each BU submits revenue/cost plans. FP&A challenges, consolidates. Zero-based budgeting (ZBB) vs. incremental budgeting debate.

Q1–Q4: Rolling Forecast

Updated 4-Quarter View

Monthly: BU actuals vs budget. Reforecast remaining quarters. Identify variances early. Flag risk to CFO. "What do you need to hit the number?"

Variance Analysis — The Core FP&A Skill

Revenue Variance Decomposition
Total Revenue Variance = Volume Variance + Price Variance + Mix Variance Volume Variance = (Actual Volume − Budget Volume) × Budget Price Price Variance = Actual Volume × (Actual Price − Budget Price) Mix Variance = Actual Volume × Budget price × (Actual Mix − Budget Mix)
A revenue miss could be: selling same volume but at lower price (price competition), or selling less volume despite hitting price (demand issue). These require different corrective actions. FP&A's job is to separate these effects and tell the CFO which is which.
🔍 Budget Variance Case: Tata Consultancy Services FY2024 Q3
FP&A EXERCISE
Budget vs Actual: Revenue miss of ₹2,200 Cr in Q3 FY24

Decomposition:
- Volume variance: -₹900 Cr (fewer billable hours — BFSI clients in US delayed projects)
- Price variance: -₹600 Cr (rate compression on renewals; new AI tools reducing hours per project)
- Currency variance: +₹400 Cr (USD/INR appreciated 2%, partially offsetting)
- Mix variance: -₹1,100 Cr (shift from high-margin consulting to lower-margin managed services)

FP&A Recommendation to CFO:
1. Renegotiate 3 large BFSI contracts up for renewal in Q4 — accept 5% rate cut to retain volume
2. Accelerate hiring freeze (already at 3.4% attrition — lowest in 10 years) → operating leverage
3. Reforecast FY24 annual revenue: ₹232,000 Cr → ₹229,000 Cr (1.3% cut)
4. Protect EBIT margin at 24.5% through SG&A freeze despite revenue miss

Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB) vs Incremental

Zero-Based Budgeting

Justify Every Rupee

Every cost must be justified from zero each cycle. No "last year + 5%." Forces managers to defend spend. Used by Hindustan Unilever, P&G, 3G Capital portfolio companies. Typically saves 10-25% in overhead costs. Time-intensive but powerful.

Incremental Budgeting

Base + Adjustments

Use prior year actuals as baseline, adjust for growth/headcount/inflation. Faster, simpler, but perpetuates inefficiency. Builds "budget defense" culture. Common in Indian PSUs and traditional corporates. Risk: zombie costs persist for decades.

ML Techniques for Finance — Andrew Ng Framework

1. Linear Regression for Price Prediction ▼

Predict stock returns using quantitative features (factors). The classic Fama-French 3-factor model is essentially a multi-variate linear regression.

Fama-French 3-Factor Model
Rᵢ - Rf = αᵢ + β₁(Rm-Rf) + β₂·SMB + β₃·HML + εᵢ SMB = Small-Minus-Big (size premium) HML = High-Minus-Low (value premium) Rf = Risk-free rate
α (alpha) = excess return unexplained by these factors. Finding consistent positive alpha = the holy grail. Quantitative approach: regress historical returns on these factors, use residuals as trading signal.
Python: Ridge Regression Price Predictor
from sklearn.linear_model import Ridge from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler # Features: momentum, RSI, volume/price, P/E, sector features = ['momentum_20d','rsi_14','vol_price_ratio','pe_ratio','beta'] X = df[features].dropna() y = df['forward_return_20d'].loc[X.index] scaler = StandardScaler() X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X) model = Ridge(alpha=1.0) model.fit(X_train, y_train) predictions = model.predict(X_test) print(f"IC (Information Coefficient): {np.corrcoef(predictions, y_test)[0,1]:.3f}")
2. Backtesting Framework — The Most Critical Quant Skill ▼

A backtest simulates how a strategy would have performed on historical data. Pitfall: overfitting — a strategy can look amazing on historical data but fail live. Always use walk-forward testing.

Backtest Checklist
  • Survivorship bias: Include delisted stocks, not just current Nifty50
  • Look-ahead bias: Don't use data that wouldn't have been available at the time of signal
  • Transaction costs: Include 0.05% NSE transaction fee + STT + impact cost for large trades
  • Slippage: Large orders move the price — especially for small-cap strategies
  • Out-of-sample testing: Develop on 2010–2019, test on 2020–2024
Key Backtest Metrics
Sharpe Ratio = (Annual Return - Rf) / Annual Volatility [target: >1.5] Sortino Ratio = (Annual Return - Rf) / Downside Volatility [better than Sharpe] Max Drawdown = Peak-to-Trough decline in NAV [target: <20%] Calmar Ratio = Annual Return / Max Drawdown [target: >1.0] Win Rate = Winning trades / Total trades [>50% good]
3. Reinforcement Learning for Trading (Advanced) ▼

An RL agent learns to trade by interacting with a simulated market environment. It receives rewards for profitable actions and learns optimal position sizing and entry/exit timing.

RL Framework
  • State: Price features, portfolio position, account value, time features
  • Action: Buy (X shares), Sell (Y shares), Hold
  • Reward: Daily P&L adjusted for risk (Sharpe-penalized reward)
  • Algorithm: PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) from OpenAI Gym
  • Used by: Citadel's Tactical Trading, Two Sigma's Venn platform

Core Concepts
  • Distributed Ledger: Copies exist on thousands of nodes simultaneously — no single point of failure or control
  • Immutability: Each block contains the hash of the previous block → altering any historical transaction requires re-mining all subsequent blocks (computationally infeasible)
  • Consensus Mechanism: PoW (Proof of Work = Bitcoin) requires solving a hash puzzle. PoS (Proof of Stake = Ethereum 2.0) = validators stake ETH as collateral
  • Smart Contracts: Self-executing code on Ethereum. Powers DeFi, NFTs, DAOs

Crypto Financial Analysis

Metrics for Crypto Analysts
  • Network Value to Transactions (NVT): Market Cap / 30-day avg on-chain transaction volume = P/E equivalent for crypto
  • Stock-to-Flow (S2F): Existing supply / annual new supply. Bitcoin S2F doubles every halving → historically predicts price expansion
  • Realized Cap: Each coin valued at price when last moved. Realized Cap < Market Cap = underwater holders
  • SOPR (Spent Output Profit Ratio): >1 = coins being sold at profit. Leading sentiment indicator

Finance Interview Prep — By Role

Actual case study formats from 2026 finance interviews across Equity Research, IB, Credit Analysis, and Corp Finance.

Top Technical Questions — Every Role

Q1Walk me through a DCF model. [IB, PE, Equity Research]

Answer structure: "A DCF values a business by the present value of its future free cash flows. Step 1: Forecast FCFF for 5-10 years by projecting revenue, EBITDA margin, D&A, capex, and working capital changes. Step 2: Calculate WACC using CAPM for cost of equity and after-tax cost of debt, weighted by target capital structure. Step 3: Compute terminal value using Gordon Growth Model (FCF × (1+g) / (WACC-g)) — where g should not exceed long-run GDP growth. Step 4: Discount all cash flows including TV to present value at WACC. Step 5: Bridge to equity value by subtracting net debt, minorities, preferred stock, then divide by diluted shares. Step 6: Run sensitivity analysis on WACC and growth rate to present a valuation range, not a single number."

Q2If EBITDA increases by ₹100, what happens to the three statements? [All roles]

Answer: "P&L: Revenue unchanged, operating costs drop by ₹100 → EBIT +₹100 → PBT +₹100 → Net Income +₹74 (assuming 26% tax) → EPS increases. Balance Sheet: Cash +₹74 (taxes paid, rest retained) → Retained Earnings +₹74 → Assets = Liabilities + Equity balance maintained. Cash Flow Statement: CFO increases by ₹74 (assuming no WC change, since EBITDA change is non-cash cost reduction). Net change in cash = +₹74. If EBITDA change is from revenue increase, accounts receivable may also increase — need context."

Q3A company has a P/E of 8× and a competitor has P/E of 40×. Why? [Equity Research, Corp Fin]

Answer (show structured thinking): "P/E = (1-retention ratio) / (Ke - g). So a lower P/E could reflect: (1) Lower growth (g): PSU bank vs private bank — mature vs high-growth. (2) Higher risk (Ke): Higher cost of equity due to leverage, regulatory risk, or sector risk. (3) Capital structure: High-debt company has lower equity value relative to earnings (leverage discount). (4) Earnings quality: P/E of 8× could reflect low-quality earnings — high accruals, cyclically elevated margins. (5) Structural discount: Conglomerate discount, holding company discount, governance concerns. I would decompose the difference into these factors using DuPont and check whether the gap is justified or an opportunity."

Q4What is WACC and when would you use a higher/lower WACC? [IB, PE]

Answer: "WACC = (E/V)×Ke + (D/V)×Kd×(1-T) — it's the blended after-tax cost of capital representing the minimum return a firm must earn on its asset base. Higher WACC scenarios: (1) More equity in capital structure (equity more expensive than debt), (2) Higher beta = more systematic risk, (3) Higher risk-free rate (rising interest environment), (4) Distressed company = higher ERP applied. Lower WACC: Less leverage, stable regulated business (utility), lower beta, strong credit rating. In India context: I use ~13-14% WACC for IT companies (low leverage, global exposure), ~15-18% for mid-cap industrials (capex-heavy, cyclical), ~10-11% for FMCG (stable cash flows, low beta)."

Q5Credit Analyst: You get 3 companies — pick one to lend ₹500 Cr. How do you decide? [Credit Analyst]

Answer structure: "I'd use the 5Cs framework systematically. For each company: (1) Capacity: Calculate DSCR = EBITDA / (Interest + Principal). Minimum 1.25× mandatory. Look at 5-year DSCR trend — improving or deteriorating? (2) Capital: Net Debt/EBITDA — prefer <2.5×. Tangible Net Worth provides cushion. (3) Collateral: What assets can be pledged? What LTV? How liquid? (4) Character: Promoter integrity, any history of restructuring, related party transactions. (5) Conditions: Industry position, cyclicality, regulatory risk. I would also check: auditor qualifications, any RBI red flags, promoter pledging %, CIBIL/CRISIL rating, and run an Altman Z-Score to quantify distress probability. Final recommendation = highest DSCR + strongest collateral + best character = lend at competitive rate."

Q6FP&A: Your revenue is ₹500 Cr below budget. CFO asks why in 5 minutes. How do you answer? [FP&A]

Answer (demonstrate structured decomposition): "I'd structure my answer as: Price × Volume × Mix. For example: 'We missed by ₹500 Cr. Decomposed: Volume miss = ₹320 Cr — key account ABC delayed ₹200 Cr order to next quarter due to inventory destocking; Price variance = -₹80 Cr — we gave competitive discounts in pharma vertical to defend market share; Mix = -₹100 Cr — our high-margin enterprise segment grew slower than low-margin SMB segment. I've already spoken with the sales head — ₹200 Cr of ABC order likely comes in Q3. Revised forecast: ₹480 Cr miss for the year, not ₹500 Cr. We're tightening SG&A by ₹50 Cr to protect EBIT.' CFO wants: root cause, quantified impact, forward look, corrective action."

Pitching a Stock (Equity Research Interview)

📈
2-Minute Stock Pitch Framework: (1) Company overview — what it does, why it matters. (2) Investment thesis — 3 specific catalysts in next 12 months. (3) Valuation — current vs target price, key multiple. (4) Key risk — one honest bear case. (5) Conviction — why BUY vs just interesting. Example: "I'd pitch Coal India — BUY. 1. India power deficit accelerating thermal coal demand 15% through FY26. 2. No new FSA (Fuel Supply Agreement) competition for 3 years — pricing power. 3. Trading at 5× EV/EBITDA vs PSU sector 8×, 45% discount unjustified. Target ₹580 vs CMP ₹490, 18% upside + 8% dividend yield = 26% total return. Bear case: carbon policy reversal — unlikely near term. Conviction: 80%."
