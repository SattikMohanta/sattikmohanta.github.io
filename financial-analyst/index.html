01

Investment Banking (M&A Advisory / ECM / DCM)

Core accounting, valuation (DCF, comps, precedents), LBO modeling, deal process, and Excel/VBA automation.

Accounting & Statement Mechanics

3‑Statement Linkages
  • NI → Retained Earnings: Net income flows to BS equity.
  • CFS reconciles: Net income + non‑cash charges – ΔWC – Capex = cash change.
  • Interest circularity: Interest expense depends on average debt balance → circular reference.
  • Debt schedule: Revolver draws/paydowns, mandatory amortization, cash sweep.
Key Schedules
  • PP&E: Opening + Capex – D&A = Closing
  • Working capital: AR = Revenue/365 × DSO; AP = COGS/365 × DPO; Inventory = COGS/365 × DIO
  • Deferred tax & NOLs: Temporary differences, valuation allowances.
  • Stock‑based comp: Added back in CFO.

Valuation & LBO

DCF & Multiples
WACC
WACC = (E/V)·Re + (D/V)·Rd·(1−T)
Unlevered FCF
UFCF = EBIT·(1−t) + D&A − CapEx − ΔNWC
Terminal Value (Gordon)
TV = UFCFₙ × (1+g) / (WACC−g)
LBO Mechanics
  • Sources & Uses: Purchase price = Equity + Debt
  • Debt tranches: Revolver, Term Loan A/B, Senior/Sub notes
  • Cash sweep: Excess FCF repays debt
  • Returns: IRR, MOIC = Exit Equity / Entry Equity
  • Sensitivity: Entry/exit multiple, leverage, EBITDA growth

Coding Stack — Typical Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Step 1: Pull data from Bloomberg/CapIQ via Excel Add‑in (BQL, DAPI) or export to CSV. Use BDP (Bloomberg Data Point) for live data.
  • Step 2: Build 3‑statement model in Excel – separate tabs for assumptions, income, balance, CF, debt schedules.
  • Step 3: Use INDEX/MATCH and OFFSET to link schedules; set up circular reference with iterative calculation enabled.
  • Step 4: Run Data Tables for sensitivity (one‑variable and two‑variable) on WACC and growth.
  • Step 5: Format pitchbook slides – use VBA macros to auto‑update charts and tables from model outputs.
  • Step 6: Validate with team: audit formulas, check hardcodes, run stress tests.
02

M&A (Corp Dev / M&A Advisory)

Deal structuring, synergies, purchase price allocation, accretion/dilution, financing, and regulatory basics.

Deal Structuring & Synergies

Structuring
  • Consideration: Cash, stock, mixed, earnouts
  • Stock‑for‑stock: Exchange ratio determination
  • Tax implications: Tax‑free reorganizations (US Section 368)
  • Asset vs Stock deal: Step‑up in basis vs. carryover basis
Synergies & PPA
  • Revenue synergies: Cross‑sell, pricing power
  • Cost synergies: Headcount, facilities, procurement
  • Purchase price allocation (PPA): Fair value of identifiable assets → goodwill
  • Earnout: Contingent consideration valuation

Calculations

Accretion/Dilution
Pro Forma EPS = (Acquirer NI + Target NI + Synergies − Incremental Interest·(1−t)) / Pro Forma Shares
Breakeven Synergies = (Dilution before synergies) / (1−t)
Goodwill & Contribution
Goodwill = Purchase Price − Fair Value of Net Identifiable Assets
Contribution = Combined EBITDA × (Party's % of Combined) − Party's Standalone EBITDA

Coding Stack — Typical Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Step 1: Use Bloomberg or S&P Capital IQ to screen targets and gather precedent deal data.
  • Step 2: Build an accretion/dilution model in Excel — input acquirer, target financials, purchase price, financing mix.
  • Step 3: Model synergies (cost and revenue) with phased realization timelines.
  • Step 4: Run sensitivity on purchase price, synergy capture rate, and interest rates.
  • Step 5: Prepare board presentation – use PowerPoint with links to Excel model for automated updates.
  • Step 6: Coordinate with legal team on SPA provisions (reps, warranties, CPs).

Financial Planning & Analysis (FP&A)

Budgeting, rolling forecasts, variance analysis, driver‑based modeling, and dashboarding.

Budgeting & Forecasting

Methods
  • Zero‑based budgeting (ZBB): Justify every rupee from scratch
  • Incremental: Base + adjustments (less time‑consuming)
  • Driver‑based: Revenue = Volume × Price; costs tied to headcount, units
  • Rolling forecast: Update 12‑18 month view monthly/quarterly
Variance Analysis
Revenue Variance
Volume = (Actual Vol − Budget Vol) × Budget Price
Price = Actual Vol × (Actual Price − Budget Price)
Mix = Actual Vol × Budget Price × (Actual Mix − Budget Mix)

Key Metrics

MetricFormulaUse
Break‑evenFixed Costs / (Price − Variable Cost per unit)Minimum sales required
Cash Conversion CycleDIO + DSO − DPOWorking capital efficiency
Run‑rateLatest period × annualization factorShort‑term extrapolation
CAGR(End/Start)^(1/n) − 1Multi‑year growth trend

Coding Stack — Typical Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Step 1: Connect to ERP/data warehouse (Oracle, SAP) using SQL to extract actuals and historical data.
  • Step 2: Load data into Excel via Power Query – transform and clean (filter, pivot, join tables).
  • Step 3: Build driver‑based forecast model using Power Pivot/DAX for large datasets.
  • Step 4: Produce monthly variance reports – use conditional formatting to flag >10% variances.
  • Step 5: Automate recurring reports with Python (pandas, openpyxl) – schedule via cron/Windows Task Scheduler.
  • Step 6: Publish dashboards in Power BI/Tableau for executive review.

Equity Research

Financial statement analysis, earnings models, valuation, investment thesis, stock pitch, and industry research.

Fundamental Analysis

Key Ratios
  • ROE (DuPont): Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage
  • ROIC: NOPAT / Invested Capital → compare to WACC for value creation
  • Leverage: Debt/EBITDA, EBITDA/Interest
  • Quality: CFO vs Net Income, accruals, pension assumptions
Modeling
  • Revenue build: By segment/geography, with volume/price drivers
  • Earnings model: Quarterly consensus, beat/miss tracking
  • Valuation: DCF, comps, sum‑of‑parts
  • Credit variant: Leverage ratios, covenant headroom, recovery analysis

Stock Pitch Framework

📊 2‑Minute Stock Pitch Structure
EQUITY RESEARCH
1. Company overview: What they do, why it matters.
2. Investment thesis: 3 specific catalysts in next 12 months.
3. Valuation: Current vs target price, key multiple.
4. Key risk: Honest bear case.
5. Conviction: Why BUY vs just interesting.
Example: "Coal India — BUY. 1. Power deficit accelerating thermal coal demand 15% through FY26. 2. No new FSA competition for 3 years – pricing power. 3. Trading at 5× EV/EBITDA vs PSU sector 8×, 45% discount. Target ₹580 vs ₹490, 18% upside + 8% dividend = 26% total return. Bear: carbon policy reversal – unlikely. Conviction: 80%."

Coding Stack — Typical Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Step 1: Use Bloomberg/Reuters to download historical financials (IS/BS/CF) – export to Excel.
  • Step 2: Build earnings model – revenue by segment, expense drivers, balance sheet assumptions.
  • Step 3: Run DCF and comps valuation – use Excel Data Tables for sensitivity.
  • Step 4: Write research note in Word/PowerPoint – include charts linked to model.
  • Step 5: Use Python (yfinance, pandas) to automate data pulls for periodic updates (weekly/monthly).

Prop Trading / Quant

Probability & statistics, derivatives pricing, backtesting, signal research, and Python-based systematic strategies.

Quantitative Foundations

Stats & Time‑Series
  • Distributions: Normal, log‑normal, t‑distribution
  • Hypothesis testing: t‑test, chi‑square, ANOVA
  • Stationarity: ADF test, cointegration for pairs trading
  • Factor models: Fama‑French, PCA
Options & Greeks
Black‑Scholes Call
C = S·N(d₁) − K·e^(−rT)·N(d₂)
Key Greeks
Δ = N(d₁), Γ = N'(d₁)/(S·σ√T), Vega = S·√T·N'(d₁)

Backtesting & Risk Metrics

Metrics
  • Sharpe: (Rp − Rf) / σp (target >1.5)
  • Sortino: (Rp − Rf) / Downside Deviation
  • VaR / CVaR: Tail risk measures
  • Max Drawdown: Peak‑to‑trough decline
  • Kelly Criterion: Optimal position sizing
Backtest Pitfalls
  • Survivorship bias: Include delisted stocks
  • Look‑ahead bias: Use only data available at signal time
  • Transaction costs: Include NSE fees, STT, impact cost
  • Out‑of‑sample: Train 2010–2019, test 2020–2024

Coding Stack — Typical Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Step 1: Collect market data (NSE/BSE tick-by-tick or daily) via APIs (yfinance, Bloomberg, or data vendors).
  • Step 2: Clean and preprocess data in Python (pandas) — handle missing, align timestamps, adjust for splits/dividends.
  • Step 3: Develop signal (e.g., momentum, mean reversion) – use numpy/scipy for calculations.
  • Step 4: Backtest using vectorbt or backtrader – include transaction costs and slippage.
  • Step 5: Analyze performance – compute Sharpe, max DD, win rate, and walk‑forward validation.
  • Step 6: Deploy strategy in live environment with risk limits (e.g., position sizing via Kelly).

Strategy Consulting

Problem‑solving frameworks (MECE, issue trees), market sizing, business case, unit economics, and case interview methodology.

Problem‑Solving Frameworks

Core Tools
  • MECE: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive
  • Issue trees: Hypothesis‑driven problem decomposition
  • Porter's Five Forces: Industry attractiveness
  • BCG Growth‑Share: Portfolio strategy
  • Pyramid Principle: Answer‑first communication
Market Sizing & Business Case
  • Top‑down: Total market × penetration
  • Bottom‑up: Unit economics × volume
  • Unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC, payback
  • NPV/ROI: Investment justification with sensitivity

Key Calculations

Unit Economics
LTV = Average Revenue per User × Gross Margin × Retention Period
CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Cost / New Customers Acquired
Payback Period = CAC / (ARPU × Margin)
Business Case
NPV = Σ CFt / (1+r)^t − Initial Investment
Break‑even Units = Fixed Costs / (Price − Variable Cost per unit)

Coding Stack — Typical Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Step 1: Structure problem – build issue tree in PowerPoint/Miro.
  • Step 2: Gather data – use SQL to query internal databases, or Excel for client-provided data.
  • Step 3: Build financial model in Excel – revenue build, cost structure, sensitivity.
  • Step 4: Synthesize insights – create charts and tables in PowerPoint.
  • Step 5: For analytics‑heavy projects, use Python (pandas, matplotlib) to analyse large datasets and generate visualisations.

Guesstimates & Company Evaluations

Market sizing, estimation techniques, and real‑life company valuation scenarios – essential for case interviews and consulting roles.

Market Sizing Techniques

Top‑Down Approach
  • Total Addressable Market (TAM): Total revenue opportunity if 100% market share.
  • SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): Portion of TAM your product/service can target.
  • SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): Realistic share you can capture given competition.
  • Example: India EV market – TAM = Total vehicles × avg price; SAM = 2‑wheeler segment; SOM = 10% share based on brand strength.
Bottom‑Up Approach
  • Unit economics: Estimate demand through individual units (customers, stores, etc.).
  • Formula: Volume = Number of outlets × average daily customers × average ticket size.
  • Best for: Retail, FMCG, consumer services where unit-level data is available.
  • Example: Revenue of a coffee chain = Number of stores × avg customers/day × avg spend/customer.
🧮
Guesstimate Formula Sheet: Always use: Population × penetration × frequency × price for consumer markets. For B2B: Number of firms × adoption rate × average contract value. Cross‑check with sanity checks – if the result implies every person buys 10 cups of coffee a day, it's wrong.

Bloomberg & ET Terminal Mastery

How analysts use the terminal for real‑time data, analytics, and execution – covering key functions and workflows.

Bloomberg Terminal – Essential Functions

Data & Analytics
  • BDP (Bloomberg Data Point): Pull a single data point (e.g., AAPL last price) into Excel. Syntax: =BDP("AAPL US Equity","LAST_PRICE")
  • BDH (Bloomberg Data History): Pull historical time‑series data (e.g., daily closing prices for 5 years).
  • BQ (Bloomberg Query): Use BQL language for more complex queries (e.g., get all stocks in Nifty50 with P/E & ROE).
  • Screen (SRCH): Screen stocks, bonds, or indices based on custom criteria (e.g., EV/EBITDA < 10, ROE > 15%).
  • DL (Data License): For bulk data exports to internal databases.
Deal & Company Research
  • DES (Company Description): Key stats, business overview, peers.
  • FA (Financial Analysis): Income, balance sheet, cash flow – with automated ratios.
  • RV (Relative Valuation): Compare company multiples vs peers immediately.
  • W (World Watch): Monitor global market headlines and news.
  • ANR (Analyst Recommendations): Consensus ratings, price targets, earnings estimates.

Bloomberg – Typical Analyst Workflow

Operational Steps
  • Morning: Check markets (W) for global macro headlines; review key currency (CURNCY) and bond yields (YC).
  • Data Collection: Use BDP/BDH in Excel to pull QoQ financials for the coverage list.
  • Valuation: Use RV to benchmark comps; export EV/EBITDA multiples to Excel for valuation models.
  • News & Event Monitoring: Set alerts (ALRT) for earnings announcements, M&A rumors, or rating changes.
  • Deal Execution: Use IB (Investment Banking) function for M&A league tables and precedent deal details.
  • Presentations: Copy charts from BI (Bloomberg Intelligence) into PowerPoint for client pitches.

ET Terminal (Economic Times) – Indian Market Focus

ET Terminal Features
  • Market Data: Live NSE/BSE prices, indices, sectoral performance.
  • Corporate Actions: Dividends, bonus, splits, rights issues.
  • Financials: 10‑year historical data for all BSE500 companies.
  • Valuation: Pre‑built DCF and comps tools for quick analysis.
  • News & Research: Indian brokerage reports, regulatory filings (SEBI), earnings call transcripts.
  • Screeners: Filter stocks by market cap, P/E, dividend yield, etc.
  • Integration: Export data to Excel for further modeling – used by many Indian analysts as a Bloomberg alternative.
📊
Pro Tip: Combine Bloomberg's global data with ET Terminal's India‑specific granularity. For Indian companies, ET Terminal often has more detailed segment reporting. Use Bloomberg for global comps and macro data.

Finance Interview Prep

Step‑by‑step walkthroughs for each role, with integration of BA/DA skills (Python, SQL, Data Viz) to stand out.

Role‑Specific Walkthroughs

📈 Investment Banking / M&A ▼

Technical prep: 3‑statement model, DCF, comps, LBO, accretion/dilution. Practice on real companies (e.g., Tata Motors, HDFC Bank).

Coding integration: Use Python (yfinance, pandas) to automate data pulls; create a script that downloads financials and computes key ratios. This shows efficiency.

Interview flow: 1) Accounting questions (linkages), 2) Valuation case (build a DCF in 30 mins), 3) Deal discussion (e.g., "Why would Company A buy Company B?").

BA/DA edge: Bring a GitHub repo with your automated data pipeline – interviewers will notice.

📊 FP&A / Corp Finance ▼

Technical prep: Budgeting, variance analysis, driver‑based modeling, 13‑week cash flow, KPI dashboards.

Coding integration: SQL is critical – practice joins, window functions, and CTEs on a financial dataset. Build a Power BI dashboard that tracks budget vs actuals.

Interview flow: 1) "Walk me through a budget cycle," 2) Variance case (given a revenue miss, decompose drivers), 3) "How would you improve working capital?"

BA/DA edge: Show a Python script that auto‑generates a monthly variance report from SQL data – this is the new standard.

📉 Equity Research ▼

Technical prep: Financial analysis, earnings models, valuation (DCF & comps), stock pitch practice.

Coding integration: Use Python to scrape earnings call transcripts, perform sentiment analysis, or track estimate revisions automatically.

Interview flow: 1) "Pitch a stock in 2 minutes," 2) Model a company's Q2 earnings, 3) "What's your view on the sector?"

BA/DA edge: Build a dashboard of your coverage universe with key metrics – interviewers love visual thinking.

🤖 Prop Trading / Quant ▼

Technical prep: Probability, time‑series, options pricing, backtesting, ML for trading.

Coding integration: Python is essential – build a backtest framework, implement a simple momentum strategy, calculate Sharpe and max drawdown.

Interview flow: 1) "Explain Black‑Scholes," 2) "How would you backtest a strategy?", 3) "Here's a dataset – find a trading signal."

BA/DA edge: Show a Jupyter notebook with a complete strategy development pipeline – from data ingestion to performance report.

📋 Strategy Consulting ▼

Technical prep: Market sizing, business case, unit economics, break‑even, case interview frameworks.

Coding integration: SQL for client data analysis; Python for statistical analysis (regression, clustering) if the practice is analytics‑heavy.

Interview flow: 1) "Estimate the market size for EV charging in India," 2) "A client wants to enter a new market – build a business case," 3) "How would you improve this company's margins?"

BA/DA edge: Present a well‑structured Excel model with clear assumptions and sensitivity – consulting interviews love clear logic.

Integrating BA/DA in Finance Interviews

🔗
How to position BA/DA skills: In every technical question, mention how you'd automate or improve the process using data skills. For example, "I'd use SQL to pull the data directly, then run a Python script to compute all ratios and flag outliers." This shows you're not just a modeler – you're a builder. Many firms (especially in India) are actively hiring analysts who can bridge finance and data.
